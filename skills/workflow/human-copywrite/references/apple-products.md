# Apple Products

Apple hardware product names: iPhone, iPad, Mac, MacBook, Apple Watch, AirPods, HomePod, AirTag, Apple Pencil, Magic Keyboard, Apple Silicon chips, and related hardware.

## Entries

### AirMac
Name for AirPort in Japan: *AirMac wireless networking*, *AirMac Extreme Base Station*, *AirMac Express Base Station*, and so on.

### AirPods
In general references, don't use *the* with *AirPods*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK. For a specific pair of AirPods, *the* is OK.

> Connect AirPods to a different device.
> Use Find My to locate your AirPods.
> Clean the AirPods you just tested, and then return them to the customer.

Name AirPods models by number, not *generation*: *AirPods Pro 2*, not *AirPods Pro (2nd generation)*.

For a specific AirPods model, use the model name: *Learn how to clean your AirPods Max*. Use just *AirPods* if content applies to any model: *Use AirPods to make and answer calls with your iPhone.*

Avoid singular *AirPod*; rewrite the sentence instead. If you do use it, put the plural form nearby, and don't use it in a prominent location, such as a heading.

> Avoid: You can change how each AirPod responds when you press or tap it.
> Preferable: You can change how each of your AirPods responds when you press or tap it.

### AirPort
Versions include *AirPort*, *AirPort Extreme*, and *AirPort Time Capsule*. Use *AirPort* for Apple's wireless networking products or technologies generally, or for the AirPort product family: *your AirPort network*, *AirPort software*.

- *Software:* AirPort software includes AirPort Utility and AirPort Disk Utility. Don't precede these app names with *the*.
- *Hardware:* AirPort hardware includes AirPort Express Base Station, AirPort Extreme Base Station, and AirPort Time Capsule. OK to shorten *AirPort Express Base Station* to *AirPort Express*, but don't use *AirPort Extreme* unless you mean the technology. Use an article or *your* with *AirPort Express*. Lowercase *base station* without the full product name.

> Correct: The AirPort Extreme Base Station is an Apple product.
> Correct: Avoid placing the base station near sources of interference.
> Incorrect: Avoid placing the Base Station near sources of interference.

### Apple
Official company name: *Apple Inc.* Use it in copyright notices, credit lines, and communications needing the legal name, such as legal documents, contracts, and forms.

> © 2025 Apple Inc. All rights reserved.

In corporate and marketing communications and the corporate address, use *Apple*. *Apple* can also mean the company's products and services.

> Today Apple announced a new MacBook computer.
> Apple displays offer a brilliant view of your work.

Don't use *Apple* alone for products or services; always add a noun: *an Apple computer*, not *an Apple*; *Apple computers*, not *Apples*; *your Apple computer's screen*, not *your Apple's screen*.

Possessive form is OK for the company.

> AirPort Extreme is Apple's wireless networking technology.

Hyphenated form is also OK.

> Apple-approved video card, Apple-certified parts, Apple-designed templates

### Apple Pencil
In general references, don't use *the* with *Apple Pencil*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK. For a specific Apple Pencil, *the* is OK.

> You can use Apple Pencil to write in any text field.
> Find the serial number of your Apple Pencil Pro.

### Apple Remote
Use for the remote control compatible with Mac, iOS and iPadOS devices, and Apple TV (2nd and 3rd generations).

For the remote used with Apple TV HD and Apple TV 4K, use *Siri Remote*. (In countries or regions where Siri isn't available for use with Apple TV, use *Apple TV Remote* instead.)

See also [Apple TV Remote](#apple-tv-remote); [Siri Remote](#siri-remote).

### Apple silicon
For Mac models with Apple silicon, say *Mac with Apple silicon*; don't say *Apple silicon Mac*. For other Mac models, use *Intel-based Mac* or *other Mac computers*.

> If you have a Mac with Apple silicon…
> If you have an Intel-based Mac…
> For other Mac computers…

In content that often compares the two computer types, *Apple silicon* can be shorthand for *a Mac with Apple silicon*.

> Create a version of your app that runs on both Apple silicon and Intel-based Mac computers.
> Apple silicon runs all performance tools found on Intel-based Mac computers.

See also [M-series chips](#m-series-chips).

### Apple T2 Security Chip
Note capitalization. After first reference, OK to shorten to *T2 chip*.

> First reference: The Apple T2 Security Chip brings a new level of integration and security to the Mac.
> Subsequent references: The T2 chip includes a secure enclave coprocessor that provides new encrypted storage capabilities.

### Apple TV
In general references, don't use *the* with *Apple TV*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK. For a specific Apple TV, *the* is OK.

> Apple TV goes to sleep after a period of inactivity.
> Find the model number of your Apple TV.
> Make sure the HDMI cable is connected to the Apple TV you're troubleshooting.

Don't abbreviate *Apple TV* as *ATV*. In text, don't write *Apple TV* as the Apple symbol plus *TV*.

> Correct: Get started with your Apple TV.
> Incorrect: Get started with your TV.

### Apple TV Remote

### Apple Vision Pro
Always use the full name. In general references, don't use *the* with *Apple Vision Pro*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK: *Adjust the fit of your Apple Vision Pro*.

You *put on* and *take off* Apple Vision Pro. When it's on, you're *wearing* it.

> Put on Apple Vision Pro and adjust the fit.
> Don't run while you're wearing Apple Vision Pro.

In text, don't write *Apple Vision Pro* as the Apple symbol plus *Vision Pro*.

> Correct: Get started with Apple Vision Pro.
> Incorrect: Get started with [Apple]Vision Pro.

Don't call Apple Vision Pro a *headset*. Usually use the product name; where the name repeats often, *device* is OK.

### Apple Watch
In general references, don't use *the* with *Apple Watch*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK. For a specific Apple Watch, *the* is OK.

> Learn how to add a workout playlist to Apple Watch.
> Mark your Apple Watch as lost.
> Turn on the Apple Watch you want to pair.

Usually use the full name. Just *watch* is OK if context makes Apple Watch clear. Don't use *Watch*.

> You can pair more than one Apple Watch with your iPhone and then customize each watch with its own settings.

Always use full Apple Watch model names: *Apple Watch Ultra*, not *Ultra*.

### Apple Watch app
iPhone app that manages Apple Watch settings. The app icon may say *Watch*, but don't call the app *the Watch app*; use the full name: *the Apple Watch app*.

### batteries
Describe batteries by number required, size, and type.

> lithium-ion battery, lithium polymer battery
> eight AAA alkaline batteries
> one AA rechargeable nickel-cadmium battery
> two nickel-metal-hydride batteries

Abbreviate battery type only if space is limited. Abbreviations include:

> NiCd (nickel-cadmium)
> Li (lithium), Li-ion (lithium-ion)
> NiMH (nickel-metal-hydride)

### Digital Crown
Precede with *the*. Always use the full term, not *Crown* or *crown*. You *turn* the Digital Crown; you don't *rotate* it.

> Correct: Turn the Digital Crown to adjust the volume.
> Incorrect: Rotate the crown to adjust the volume.

Use *press* for pressing the Digital Crown once; *double-click* or *triple-click* for pressing it more than once.

### Fit Dial

### iPad
In general references, don't use *the* with *iPad*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK. For a specific iPad, *the* is OK.

> Rotate iPad to landscape orientation.
> Find the serial number of your iPad.
> Select the iPad you want to use as a second display.

- *Chip or generation:* Models are identified by chip (newer models) or generation. Chip formats for general text:

> iPad Pro with the M4 chip is available for purchase.
> iPad Pro with M4 is available for purchase.
> iPad Pro (M4) is available for purchase.

Generation can go before or after the name; use the numeral when it follows.

> The fifth-generation iPad Air was announced in March 2022.
> iPad Air (5th generation) features a Liquid Retina display and the Apple M1 chip.

Don't shorten *generation* to *gen* or *G*—for example, *10th-gen iPad* or *iPad 10G*.

Formats for listing multiple generations:

> iPad (5th generation or later)
> iPad (9th and 10th generation)
> iPad Pro (2nd, 3rd, and 4th generation)

- *Display size:* Display size can go before or after the name: *12.9-inch iPad Pro*, *iPad Pro (12.9-inch)*. With both display size and chip (or generation) in general text, list display size first.
- *Capacity:* Capacity can go before or after the name: *512 GB iPad Pro*, *iPad Pro 512 GB*.

See also [generation](numbers-and-dates.md#generation); [M-series chips](#m-series-chips).

### iPhone
In general references, don't use *the* with *iPhone*. Another article or a possessive adjective is OK. For a specific iPhone, *the* is OK.

> To lock iPhone, press the side button.
> You can change the name of your iPhone.
> Turn off the iPhone you're troubleshooting, and then press and hold the side button to turn the device back on.

Capacity can go before or after the name.

> iPhone 14 (128 GB, 256 GB, and 512 GB) includes…
> The 1 TB iPhone 15 Pro features the A17 Pro chip.

Don't shorten iPhone to *phone*; always use *iPhone* or the specific model name (*iPhone 14*, *iPhone 15 Pro Max*, and so on).

> Correct: Tap Level, and then hold iPhone against an object, such as a picture frame.
> Incorrect: Tap Level, and then hold the phone against an object, such as a picture frame.

For iPhone XR, iPhone XS, and iPhone XS Max, use small caps for R and S if tools allow; otherwise uppercase R and S. Don't use lowercase.

> Preferable: iPhone XR, iPhone XS, iPhone XS Max
> Acceptable (in case of tools limitations): iPhone XR, iPhone XS, iPhone XS Max
> Incorrect: iPhone Xr, iPhone Xs, iPhone Xs Max

Letter X in the model name is pronounced "ten" (for example, "iPhone ten-ess").

### iPod
Last iPod model is iPod touch. (Previous models include iPod classic, iPod nano, and iPod shuffle.) Always include *iPod* in the name; don't shorten to *touch* alone.

In general references, don't use an article with *iPod touch*. For the user's particular iPod touch, *your* is OK. Capacity and generation can go before or after the name: *128 GB iPod touch*, *iPod touch (128 GB)*, *sixth-generation iPod touch*, *iPod touch (6th generation)*. Use the numeral when generation follows the model name. Don't shorten *generation* to *gen* or *G*; for example, *seventh-gen iPod touch* or *iPod touch 7G*.

> The sixth-generation iPod touch features the Apple A8 chip.
> If you experience problems with your iPod touch (7th generation)…

Don't capitalize *touch* even in title-style headings: *iPod touch*, not *iPod Touch*.

### Mac
Use *Mac* for Mac computers and related products (*Mac software*, *Mac apps*).

- *Articles:* In general references, don't use *the* with *Mac* or specific Mac model names.

> Get to know Mac.
> Mac mini delivers unprecedented power in a small package.
> Mac Pro features seven expansion slots.

Another article or a possessive adjective is OK.

> This feature requires a Mac with macOS 14 or later.
> Set up your Mac Studio.

For a specific Mac, *the* is OK.

> Shut down the Mac you want to restore.
> Slide the Mac Pro you're troubleshooting out of the rack.

- *Plurals and possessives:* *Mac* and specific Mac model names are trademarks, so don't use them in plural or possessive form.

> Correct: Mac computers, Mac Pro models, your Mac computer's screen
> Incorrect: Macs, Mac Pros, your Mac's screen

- *Generic references:* Don't call a Mac computer a *machine*, *CPU*, or *PC*; acceptable alternatives are *computer* or *personal computer*.

Ways to list Mac computers with other product types:

> your Mac, iPhone, or iPad; a macOS, iOS, or iPadOS device; your Mac or mobile device; your Mac or PC; Mac computers and PCs

See also [device](apple-features-software.md#device); [trademarks (usage)](punctuation.md#trademarks-usage).

### M-series chips
Series of systems on a chip (SoCs) built on Apple silicon. Each set of M-series chips can be called a *family* (*the M3 family of chips*, *the M4 family*). M-series chips include these families:

- M1 (M1, M1 Pro, M1 Max, and M1 Ultra)
- M2 (M2, M2 Pro, M2 Max, and M2 Ultra)
- M3 (M3, M3 Pro, M3 Max, and M3 Ultra)
- M4 (M4, M4 Pro, and M4 Max)

Chip name is OK with or without *Apple*.

> The Apple M2 chip extends battery life while providing increased performance.
> MacBook Pro with the M3 Max chip supports extreme workflows and multitasking across pro apps.

OK to omit *chip* when referring to M-series chips repeatedly.

> M3, M3 Pro, and M3 Max bring massive enhancements and new features to the Mac.
> Like M4 Pro, M4 Max supports Thunderbolt 5 at transfer speeds of up to 120 Gbit/s.

For devices with an M-series chip, use terms such as *Mac with the M4 chip* and *iPad Pro with M4*; don't use *M4 Mac*, *M4 iPad*, or other variations. Don't call an M-series chip a *processor*.

See also [Apple silicon](#apple-silicon).

### drive
Use for a data storage device: hard drive (holds magnetic disks), optical drive (holds optical discs), or solid-state drive (flash memory, no moving parts). Don't capitalize *drive* except in a product name such as *SuperDrive* or *Fusion Drive*.

OK to use *drive* alone after first specifying the drive type.

> If your Mac doesn't have a built-in optical drive, you can use another computer's drive.

Don't use *drive* when you mean *disk* or *disc*.

> Correct: You can partition the disk in Disk Utility.
> Incorrect: You can partition the drive in Disk Utility.

See also [disk](apple-features-software.md#disk); [hard drive](#hard-drive).

### hard drive
Use for the mechanism that holds a magnetic disk and accesses its contents.

> You can back up your Mac using an external hard drive.

More formal *hard disk drive* is also OK, especially where precision matters.

For storage media broadly, including solid-state drives and flash storage, use *storage device*.

> You can connect an external storage device to your computer.

See also [drive](#drive); [hard disk (n., adj.)](#hard-disk-n-adj).

### hard disk (n., adj.)
Use for a magnetic disk.

> The files are stored on the server's hard disk.

For storage media broadly, including solid-state drives and flash storage, use just *disk* or a broader term such as *storage device*.

> You can use Disk Utility to manage internal disks and external storage devices.

See also [hard drive](#hard-drive); [storage device](apple-features-software.md#storage-device).
