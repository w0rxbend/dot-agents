# Voice and Tone

Apple Style Guide entries on verb usage, tense, voice (active vs passive), addressing the user, contractions, and tone.

## Entries

### abort
Avoid in user materials. See also [exit](#exit); [interrupt](#interrupt); [quit](#quit); [stop](#stop).

### access (n., v.)
Verb *access* is OK when you can't be more specific. Even in developer materials, consider more precise terms, such as *log in to*.

> Avoid: Access the server using an administrator account.
> Preferable: Log in to the server using an administrator account.
> Avoid: You can access the internet with your MacBook Air.
> Preferable: You can connect to the internet with your MacBook Air.

### activate, deactivate
Avoid; use *turn on, turn off*.

### active
Use for app or window currently in use. Preferred to *in front*. See also [front, frontmost](#front-frontmost); [in front](#in-front).

### allow
Avoid *allow* when restructuring can make the reader the subject.

> Avoid: FileMaker Pro allows you to create a database.
> Preferable: You can create a database with FileMaker Pro.

See also [enable (v.), enabled (adj.)](#enable-v-enabled-adj); [let](#let).

### appear
Use *appear*, not *display*, for items becoming visible onscreen.

> Correct: The setup assistant appears.
> Incorrect: The setup assistant is displayed.
> Incorrect: The setup assistant displays.

In a task, avoid stating that an item *appears*. If clarity needs it, work it into task context.

> 1. Click the close button.
> 2. In the message that appears, click Save.

OK to use *appear* in a task for information like this:

> The printer icon appears only when there are jobs in the printing queue.
> Photos, videos, and any other files appear in a grid.

### assure
Don't use when you mean *ensure*. *Assure* means to state positively, to remove doubt.

> Assure customers that you'll resolve the issue.

See also [ensure, insure](word-choice-and-confusables.md#ensure-insure).

### attach
Don't use to mean *connect* (as in *Connect the USB device to your computer*).

### authenticate, authorize
Apple devices and services *authenticate* users to confirm their identity.

> Signing in with AppleConnect authenticates your identity and gives you access to Apple's internal apps and services.

Users *authorize* payments with methods such as a passcode. Users also *authorize* or *deauthorize* computers and devices. Banks and merchants *authorize* transactions after confirming customer has funds for purchase.

> To prevent unauthorized purchases, use Screen Time settings.
> To play some iTunes Store purchases, you must authorize your computer.

See also [code, passcode](word-choice-and-confusables.md#code-passcode).

### can, might, may
Use *can* for capacity to do something. Use *might* or *may* for possibility. Use *may* for permission.

> You can log in to your account from another computer.
> You might be able to connect to the internet at a nearby hotspot.
> You may borrow my iPad if you return it tomorrow.

For possibility, *might* typically suggests lower probability than *may*.

> If the issue persists, you may need to restart your computer or you might need to restore software.

### capability
If possible, avoid *capability* for software or hardware features. Reword as what user can do with the feature.

> Correct: With Photos, you can create slideshows.
> Incorrect: Photos has the capability to create slideshows.

### choose
Use *choose*, not *select*, for menu items. Generally, user *selects* something (such as file or disk icon, email message, or section of text), then *chooses* a command to act on it.

> Choose View > Sort By > Date.
> Select the text to copy, and then choose Edit > Copy.
> Click the pop-up menu, and then choose High Priority.
> Control-click the TextEdit icon, and then choose Make Alias.

See also [menus](ui-elements.md#menus); [select (v.)](#select-v).

### click
Use for selecting something or starting an action: position pointer over onscreen element, briefly press and release mouse or trackpad.

> To open Mail, click the Mail icon in the Dock.
> Click a disk icon to select it, and then choose File > Make Alias.

Don't use *click on*. Don't say *click the mouse* or *click the trackpad*; use *press and release*.

> Press the mouse, drag to enclose the area you want to select, and then release.

See also [click and hold](#click-and-hold); [click in](#click-in); *double click (n.), double-click (v.), double-clicking (n., v.)*; [press](#press); [select (v.)](#select-v); [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v); *triple click (n.), triple-click (v.), triple-clicking (n., v.)*.

### click and drag
Don't use. You either *click* or *drag*.

> Correct: Drag the icon to the Trash.
> Incorrect: Click and drag the icon to the Trash.

### click and hold
Positioning pointer on onscreen item and pressing mouse or trackpad until something happens.

> Click the Previous or Next button to go to the previous or next clip, or click and hold the Previous or Next button to rewind or fast-forward.

Don't use *click and hold* for pressing deeper on a Force Touch trackpad; use *force click*.

See also [click](#click); [force click (v.)](#force-click-v); [hold down](#hold-down); [press](#press); [touch and hold](#touch-and-hold).

### click in
You *click in* a window or other screen area, such as an image; you *click* other onscreen elements, such as icons and buttons. See also [click](#click).

### click on
Don't use; use *click*. See also [click](#click).

### close
In macOS, users *close* windows or documents and *quit* apps.

In iOS, users can *close* an app (stop using it temporarily) by pressing the Home button. This may not *quit* the app (stop it running altogether). Also OK: *leave* for closing an app.

> To leave an app and return to the Home Screen, press the Home button.

See also [open](#open); [quit](#quit).

### connect
Use for joining devices; don't use *attach*, *hook up*, or *mate*. You *connect* USB devices to a computer and *connect* computers to a network. Don't use *connect* when you mean *plug in*. See also [plug in (v.), plug into (v.)](word-choice-and-confusables.md#plug-in-v-plug-into-v).

### contractions
Apple's informal voice uses and recommends contractions in most documentation, interface text, and marketing copy. Consider localization when deciding how or whether to use them.

- Use common contractions of be-verbs and auxiliary verbs with *not* (*aren't, isn't, can't, couldn't, didn't, doesn't, hadn't, haven't, weren't, won't*).

> You don't have access to the information.
> The error shouldn't occur again.

- Use common contractions of be-verbs and auxiliary verbs with personal pronouns (*he's, I'm, I've, it's, she'll, they're, we've, you're*). Also OK: *here's, let's, that's, there's,* and *what's*.

> Here's a quick look at what's new in this release.
> We're building a solar farm in China.

- Don't form contractions from nouns or proper nouns.

> Avoid: The computer's not working.
> Preferable: The computer isn't working.
> Avoid: Apple's going to introduce a new computer today.
> Preferable: Apple is going to introduce a new computer today.

- Avoid contractions of multipart verbs (*could've*), colloquialisms (*workin'*), and other contractions that are uncommon or awkward in writing, or may confuse nonnative English speakers (*everybody'd, how're, it'll, many's, when's, where'll, who've, why's*). Possible exceptions: text meant to be spoken (such as a script) and direct quotes in running text.

- Don't confuse *it's* (contraction of *it is*) with *its* (possessive pronoun).

> It's important for the department to keep track of its computers.

See also [possessives](punctuation.md#possessives).

### corrupted
Avoid if possible. Use *damaged*.

### crash
Don't use; use *quits unexpectedly*, *doesn't respond*, or *stops responding*. If you must use it, put it in quotation marks and reassure reader that *crash* doesn't imply hardware or software damage.

### depress
Don't use; use *press*.

### desire
Don't use. See also [want](#want).

### desired
Try to avoid.

> Correct: make your changes, select the folder
> Incorrect: make the desired changes, select the desired folder

### disable (v.), disabled (adj.)
Don't use *disable* for turning off features or deselecting options; use *turn off* or *deselect*.

> You can turn off Airplane Mode using Control Center.
> To hide an object's caption, deselect the Caption checkbox.

Don't use *disabled* for features turned off or unavailable; use *turned off*, *unavailable*, or *inactive*.

> Make sure virus-protection software is turned off.
> The Copy command is unavailable if there's no text selected.
> Active roles appear in color in the timeline; inactive roles appear in gray.

See also [enable (v.), enabled (adj.)](#enable-v-enabled-adj); [turn on, turn off](#turn-on-turn-off).

### display (v.)
Don't use *display* when you mean *appear*.

> Correct: The Print dialog appears.
> Incorrect: The Print dialog displays.

See also [appear](#appear).

### do
Don't use in phrases such as *do a clean installation*. Use *perform*.

### enable (v.), enabled (adj.)
Avoid when you mean *turn on*. *Turn on* implies starting an action or process immediately; *enable* implies a task that makes later actions possible.

> Correct: Turn on Windows file sharing.
> Incorrect: Enable Windows file sharing.

Don't use *enabled* when you mean *selected* (radio buttons or checkboxes) or *available* (commands or buttons that are sometimes dimmed, but not in this case).

> Correct: Make sure the Use Touch ID checkbox is selected in Notes settings.
> Incorrect: Make sure the Use Touch ID option is enabled in Notes settings.
> Correct: The Copy command isn't available because no text is selected.
> Incorrect: The Copy command isn't enabled because no text is selected.

Don't use *enable* when you mean *give the ability to*. Rewriting the sentence usually avoids it.

> Correct: With iMovie, you can view, edit, and share movie projects.
> Incorrect: iMovie enables you to view, edit, and share movie projects.

OK to use *enable* for a task that makes other actions possible.

> To use this website, you must enable cookies.

See also [allow](#allow); [disable (v.), disabled (adj.)](#disable-v-disabled-adj); [let](#let).

### enter
Use *enter*, not *type*, for inputting text-based information by typing, copying and pasting, dragging, or other method. Use *type* for pressing or tapping keys to produce characters onscreen. Use *press*, not *type*, for pressing keyboard keys.

> Enter your account information and tap Save.
> The intelligent keyboard may suggest corrections as you type.
> Press the Return key.

See also [press](#press); [type (v.)](#type-v).

### entitled
Don't use; use *titled*, *named*, or *called*.

### exit
In user materials, don't use for quitting an open app; use *quit*. See also [interrupt](#interrupt); [quit](#quit); [stop](#stop).

### first person
Don't use first-person pronouns *we*, *us*, or *I*; rewrite in terms of reader or product.

### force click (v.)
Use for pressing a Force Touch trackpad until you feel a deeper click.

> Force click a file's icon to see a quick preview of the file.

See also [click and hold](#click-and-hold); *Force Touch*.

### force quit
In user materials, prefer *force an app to quit*.

> Acceptable: If an app doesn't respond, you can force quit it.
> Preferable: If an app doesn't respond, you can force it to quit.

### freeze
Use for onscreen pointer behavior. Avoid *freeze* as a noun or for something the computer does. Use a phrase such as *not responding*.

> Correct: If the pointer freezes, or if the computer stops responding to the mouse, trackpad, or keyboard, follow these instructions.
> Incorrect: If the computer freezes, follow these instructions.
> Incorrect: If you experience a freeze, follow these instructions.

### front, frontmost
The active window is the *front* or *frontmost* window. See also [active](#active); [in front](#in-front).

### functionality
In user materials, avoid if a word such as *features* works instead.

> Avoid: Some functionality is not available in certain regions.
> Preferable: Some features are not available in certain regions.

### future tense
Use present, not future, tense whenever possible. Don't switch needlessly to future tense when present tense can express a sequence of steps or events.

- *Conditional constructions:* Use present tense for conditionals like this:

> Correct: If the noWait parameter is true, play from the disk stops immediately, and program control returns to the caller.
> Incorrect: If the noWait parameter is true, play from the disk will stop immediately, and program control will return to the caller.

- *Future events:* Future tense sometimes fits, for example when described product isn't available yet.

> Apple will give users a peek at the new app by releasing a public beta.
> Next year students at Clareton High School will use EPUB textbooks in their math classes.

### hang
Don't use for computer behavior in response to system error; use a phrase such as *not responding*.

> Correct: If the computer doesn't respond to input from the mouse, trackpad, or keyboard, a system error may have occurred.
> Incorrect: If the computer hangs, a system error may have occurred.

See also [freeze](#freeze).

### highlight (v.)
Don't use when you mean *select*.

> Correct: Select the text you want to change.
> Incorrect: Highlight the text you want to change.

Don't use as an intransitive verb.

> Correct: As you type in the search field, matching icons are highlighted in the window below.
> Incorrect: As you type in the search field, matching icons highlight in the window below.

See also [highlighted (adj.)](#highlighted-adj); [highlighting (n.)](#highlighting-n); [select (v.)](#select-v).

### hit (v.)
Don't use to tell users to press a key or touch a screen; use *press* or *tap*. See also [press](#press); [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v).

### hold down
Don't use for pressing mouse or trackpad, keyboard key, or mechanical button until an action or result occurs. Use *press and hold*. See also [press and hold](#press-and-hold).

### hover (v.)
Avoid *hover over* for holding pointer over onscreen element until something occurs. Use *hold the pointer over* or *move the pointer to*.

> Correct: Hold the pointer over an address, and then click the arrow that appears.
> Correct: Move the pointer to the right of Auto-Play, click the pop-up menu, and then choose an option.
> Avoid: Hover over a participant's name, and then click the More button.

Don't use *hover the pointer over*.

See also [Apple Pencil hover](apple-features-software.md#apple-pencil-hover); [Hover Text](apple-features-software.md#hover-text).

### if necessary
Avoid in user materials. Describe the circumstance that makes the action necessary.

> Correct: If file sharing isn't on, turn it on.
> Incorrect: If necessary, turn on file sharing.
> Correct: If the file size is larger than the maximum your mail app allows, compress the file.
> Incorrect: If necessary, compress the file.

### if, whether
Use *if* to indicate a condition. Use *whether* when two outcomes are possible.

If you can mentally add *or not* to a clause, use *whether*, not *if*.

> Correct: When you start editing a locked file, the app asks whether you want to unlock it.
> Incorrect: When you start editing a locked file, the app asks if you want to unlock it.

Although *if* specifies a condition, it can also create ambiguity. *Let her know if he sets up the user account* may mean either *Let her know whether he sets up the user account* or *If he sets up the user account, let her know*. Make the condition's meaning clear.

### in front
Use to explain the term *active* (*the active window is in front of other windows*); afterward, use *active*. Also use for windows that "float" on top of active windows. See also [active](#active); [front, frontmost](#front-frontmost).

### in order to
Don't use unless absolutely necessary; use just *to*.

### input (n., adj.)
Avoid as a verb; use *enter* or *type*, depending on context.

### inside
Not *inside of*.

### interrupt
OK to use as a noun in developer materials. See also [exit](#exit); [force quit](#force-quit); [quit](#quit); [stop](#stop).

### kill
Don't use for stopping an app or process. Use an alternative that conveys correct meaning in context, such as *force quit*, *force exit*, *terminate*, *end*, *stop*, *halt*, or *cancel*.

> Correct: Cancel all background tasks.
> Incorrect: Kill all background tasks.

If code you're documenting uses *kill* and can't be changed, show a code sample so users know what to enter, but use alternative terms in documentation.

### launch
Avoid in user materials when you mean to *open* an app. OK in developer materials.

### let
Don't overuse *lets you* in instructions; restructure the sentence to focus on what user does.

> Avoid: The Up Next button lets you see which songs will play next.
> Preferable: Click the Up Next button to see which songs will play next.

OK to use *lets you* in content describing features and their capabilities.

> Family Sharing lets you share Apple purchases and subscriptions with family.

See also [allow](#allow); [disable (v.), disabled (adj.)](#disable-v-disabled-adj); [enable (v.), enabled (adj.)](#enable-v-enabled-adj).

### like, love
Users can *like* or *love* an item. Items can have *likes*, but not *loves*; call loved items *items you've loved* or *loved items*. Users can *dislike* a liked or loved item. Don't capitalize *like* or *love* unless referring to a UI button (for example, *the Loved button*).

To avoid ambiguity, also OK: *mark an item as liked*, an item *is marked as loved*, or similar.

### localizable
Don't use.

> Correct: An app that follows the human interface guidelines should be easy to localize.
> Incorrect: An app that follows the human interface guidelines should be easily localizable.

### localize (v.)
Takes the preposition *for*, not *to*.

> Correct: Whether you localize your app for the French language or for French Canada, make sure you test its interface.

### look
Use for looking at buttons and elements in visionOS to interact with them. Don't use *gaze*.

> App icons subtly come to life when you look at them.

In visionOS, looking at an element is integral to interacting with it, so *look* isn't needed in every instruction. When first explaining how to do something on Apple Vision Pro, including *look* can help describe the action.

> First reference: To open an app, look at the app and tap your fingers together.
> Subsequent references: Tap an app to open it.

### mount (v.)
In user materials, avoid for making a disk or disk image available. Use alternatives such as *open*, *make available*, or *connect to*, or describe what user must do to make disk available.

> Avoid: To see the contents of a disk image, you must mount the disk image.
> Preferable: To see the contents of a disk image, you must open the disk image by double-clicking it.

In server materials or materials for more technical users, *mount* is OK.

Don't use *mount* as an intransitive verb.

> Correct: Mount the volume.
> Incorrect: The volume mounts.

See also [unmount (v.)](#unmount-v).

### native
In user materials, avoid *native* for apps; describe apps as designed to work with specific hardware or software.

> For best performance, use an app designed for use on Intel-based Mac computers.

### new
In most documents, avoid calling a product or feature new; text quickly goes out of date. When appropriate, state software version that introduced the feature.

> Correct: Auto Unlock, introduced with macOS Sierra 10.12…
> Incorrect: The new Auto Unlock feature…

### once
Don't use when you mean *after*.

> Correct: After you turn on the computer…
> Incorrect: Once you turn on the computer…

### open
Users *open* icons, folders, documents, and apps. See also [close](#close).

### optionally
Avoid in user materials. Describe why user would perform the action. To emphasize the action is optional, you can say *If you want to…*

> Correct: If you want to set a disk quota, enter the number and specify megabytes (MB) or gigabytes (GB).
> Incorrect: Optionally, enter a disk quota and specify megabytes (MB) or gigabytes (GB).

### passive voice
Avoid when possible; use active voice. Passive voice is sometimes appropriate and necessary, for example when active voice would need highly convoluted sentence structure or excessive anthropomorphism.

In tutorials, passive construction might fit to avoid miscuing the reader: when describing an action user isn't supposed to try yet.

> Explanation screen: An icon is selected by clicking it.
> User-try screen: You try it. Click the icon.

### please
Avoid *please* in instructional text and cross-references.

> Correct: Follow the steps below.
> Incorrect: Please follow the steps below.
> Correct: For more information, see "Store Settings" on page 96.
> Incorrect: For more information, please see "Store Settings" on page 96.

### point (v.)
When describing the desktop interface, avoid *point* as a verb. Use *move the pointer to* or similar wording.

> Avoid: Point to an item to see a description of what you can do with it.
> Preferable: Hold the pointer over an item to see a description of what you can do with it.

### press
Use for pressing and quickly releasing keyboard keys, mechanical buttons, and switches. Don't use *click*, *hit*, *push*, *tap*, or *type*.

> Correct: Press the Return key.
> Correct: Press the power button.
> Incorrect: Type your password and hit the Return key.

Also use *press* for pressing the stem on some AirPods models.

- Don't use *press* for onscreen items; use *click*, *force click*, or *click and hold*.

> Correct: Click and hold an icon in the Dock until a menu appears.
> Correct: Force click an address to see it on a map.
> Incorrect: Press an icon in the Dock until a menu appears.

- Don't use *press* when you mean *press and hold*.

> Correct: Press and hold the C key while you restart the computer.
> Incorrect: Press the C key and restart the computer. [It's not clear that you need to keep pressing.]

- Don't say *press and drag*; say *drag*.

See also [click](#click); [enter](#enter); [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v); [type (v.)](#type-v).

### press and hold
Use *press and hold* (not *hold down*) for pressing mouse or trackpad, keyboard key, or mechanical button until a specified action or result occurs.

Don't use *press and hold* when you mean *press*, which means to press and quickly release a key or mechanical button.

> Correct: To restart your Mac, press and hold the power button.
> Incorrect: To restart your Mac, press the power button.

Don't use *press and hold* when you mean *click and hold*. *Click and hold* means using the mouse or trackpad to interact with onscreen items; *press and hold* means interacting with the physical mouse or trackpad itself.

See also [click and hold](#click-and-hold); [press](#press); [touch and hold](#touch-and-hold).

### prompt (n., v., adj.)
Avoid *prompt* as a verb if you can tell users directly to do something, or use friendlier wording such as *when asked* or *you may be asked*.

> Avoid: Double-click the side button, and then enter your passcode when prompted.
> Preferable: Double-click the side button, and then enter your passcode.

If you do use *prompt*, don't make hardware or software the actor; use passive voice.

> Correct: If you select Image, you're prompted to select an image file.
> Incorrect: If you select Image, the app prompts you to select an image file.

OK to use *prompt* as noun or adjective for the prompt character in a command-line interface.

### push
Don't use for pressing a button or keyboard key; use *press*. See also [press](#press).

Don't use *push* for services (such as iCloud) that send content to devices automatically; say content *appears automatically* or *is updated*.

> iCloud updates your photos on all your devices.

### put
Don't use when you mean *drag*.

> Correct: Drag the file to the Trash.
> Incorrect: Put the file in the Trash.

### quit
Use *quit* for stopping an app from running completely. Don't use *exit*, *exit from*, or *leave* when you mean *quit*. See also [close](#close); [force quit](#force-quit); [interrupt](#interrupt); [stop](#stop).

### recommend
For something users are encouraged to do, don't use *we recommend* or *Apple recommends*; use *recommended*.

> Correct: It's recommended that you import video using the same camera you used to record it.
> Incorrect: We recommend that you import video using the same camera you used to record it.

Less formal phrases like *it's a good idea to* also work.

> It's a good idea to create a password hint.

### reference
Don't use as a verb; use *refer to*.

### regular
Don't use when you mean *standard*, as in *Use standard settings*.

### restart (v.)
Don't use as a noun.

### restore (v., adj.)
Don't use as a noun.

> Correct: Avoid stopping the restore process.
> Incorrect: Avoid stopping a restore in progress.
> Correct: Restoring stopped because the disk is full.
> Incorrect: The restore stopped because the disk is full.

### run (v.), running (adj.)
Use *running* for background programs or processes with no graphical interface.

> Open Activity Monitor to see what processes are running.

- *Apps:* Don't use *run* for what a user does with an app (a program with a graphical interface); say *use*.

> Correct: Before installing the software, use Disk Utility to verify your disk.
> Incorrect: Before installing the software, run Disk Utility.

Don't use *running* for an open app; use *open*.

> Correct: Check to see whether any apps are open.
> Incorrect: Check to see whether any apps are running.

- *Software versions:* In user materials, avoid *running* for operating system or app versions. Use alternatives such as *use*, *using*, or *installed*. In server materials or materials for more technical users, *running* is OK for operating system or app versions.

> Correct: If you're using iOS 12 or earlier, follow these instructions.
> Incorrect: If you're running iOS 12 or earlier, follow these instructions.

### scroll
Avoid as a transitive verb.

> Correct: Scroll through a document.
> Correct: Scroll to view more of the document.
> Incorrect: Scroll a document.

In user materials, for moving through documents, webpages, groups of options, and other items, *scroll* is often preferable to specific actions and gestures such as *drag* or *swipe*.

> Acceptable: Drag up or down to scroll through the songs in the list until you find the one you want.
> Preferable: Scroll through the songs in the list until you find the one you want.

### select (v.)
Use *select*, not *choose*, when users select among multiple objects (such as icons, graphic images, radio buttons, or checkboxes) or highlight text for editing.

> Select a name in the list.
> To select several files at once, Command-click them.
> Select the text you want to make bold.
> Select the Shadow checkbox.

Use *choose* for menu items, including those in pop-up and shortcut menus.

See also [checkbox](ui-elements.md#checkbox); [choose](#choose); [click](#click); [deselect](#deselect); [highlight (v.)](#highlight-v); [menus](ui-elements.md#menus); [unselected (adj.)](#unselected-adj).

### shows up
Don't use; use *appears*. OK to use *show* for showing and hiding onscreen elements.

> The Portrait Lighting slider appears below the frame.
> To show a hidden photo, choose View > Hidden Photos.

### slide (v.)
Avoid for how users operate a slider or switch. Use *tap*, *click*, or *drag*.

> Correct: To increase or decrease the effect, drag the slider.
> Incorrect: To increase or decrease the effect, slide the slider.
> Correct: Tap the switch to turn Airplane Mode on or off.
> Incorrect: Slide the switch to turn Airplane Mode on or off.

Other meanings of *slide* are OK in other contexts.

### start
Don't use when you mean *open* (as in *open an app*).

### stop
General term: cause a process, command, or program to cease. Don't use when you mean *quit an app*. See also [exit](#exit); [interrupt](#interrupt); [quit](#quit).

### support
Avoid in user materials when you can use *compatible*, *works with*, or another fitting word or phrase.

> Avoid: The first-generation iPad didn't support AirPlay Mirroring.
> Preferable: AirPlay Mirroring works with iPad 2 or later.
> Avoid: iMovie supports most QuickTime formats.
> Preferable: You can use files in most QuickTime formats with iMovie.

OK to use *support* for a product or feature Apple doesn't provide but the user can obtain (for example, software from other manufacturers). Avoid saying *Apple doesn't support* something.

### tap (n., v.)
On touchscreen or trackpad devices, *tap* means quickly touching and releasing touchscreen or trackpad. Don't use *tap on*.

> Correct: Tap Return to move from one field to another.
> Incorrect: Tap on the video you want to play.

Users can tap with one or more fingers (depending on device and action).

> To zoom in or out, double-tap with two fingers.

On Apple Vision Pro, *tap* means briefly tapping thumb and finger together. See also [pinch (v.)](#pinch-v).

For the Digital Touch feature, don't say users *tap* another device or person; they *send a tap*.

For haptic feedback, don't say a device or trackpad *taps you*; say *you feel a tap*.

### tap and hold
Don't use. *Tap* means to touch and release quickly, so use *touch and hold*. See also [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v); [touch and hold](#touch-and-hold).

### that
Use to introduce a restrictive clause. Clauses beginning with *that* are generally not set off with commas.

> This is the computer that has network access.
> The newest computer in the lab, which is a Mac Pro, is also the students' favorite.

See also [which](#which).

### throw away
Don't use when you mean *drag an item to the Trash*.

> Correct: After you've expanded the compressed file, drag it to the Trash.
> Incorrect: Throw away the compressed file after you expand it.

### toggle (v.)
OK in developer materials. Don't use in user materials; say *turn on or off*, *switch between*, or whatever wording fits the context.

See also [switch (v.)](word-choice-and-confusables.md#switch-v).

### touch and hold
Touching a touchscreen and leaving finger motionless until an action or result occurs.

> Touch and hold the Mute button to hold a call.

Don't use *tap and hold*. Don't use *long press* in user materials (it's OK in developer materials).

See also [click and hold](#click-and-hold); [gestures](#gestures); [long press (n.), long-press (adj.)](#long-press-n-long-press-adj); [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v).

### turn on, turn off
OK for power to a computer or peripheral device. Also use *turn on* for enabling features such as file sharing, as in *turn on file sharing*. See also *startup (n., adj.), start up (v.)*.

### type (v.)
Use for pressing keys to produce characters onscreen. Use code font or quotation marks for what user actually types, per your department's style guidelines.

> In the Title field, type Exchange Rate per $1.
> For example, type "dog" in the search field to find photos of dogs.

Use regular text font to describe generically what the user types.

> Type a new timecode value and press Return.

Don't use *type in* when you mean *type*. See also [enter](#enter); [press](#press).

### we
Don't use first person; rewrite in terms of reader or product.

> Correct: For best results, the image should be at least 600 x 600 pixels.
> Incorrect: We recommend that the image be at least 600 x 600 pixels.

See also [recommend](#recommend).

### which
Use only to introduce a nonrestrictive clause. Clauses beginning with *which* are always set off with commas.

> The newest computer in the lab, which is a Mac Pro, is also the students' favorite.
> This is the computer that has network access.

See also [that](#that).

### while, although, whereas
*While* means *during the time that* and implies concurrent activities. Use *although* to mean *in spite of the fact that*. Use *whereas* to mean *it being the fact that* or *while on the contrary*.

> Correct: Although the clip won't play back at 1x (normal) speed, every frame plays back, with no frames dropped.
> Incorrect: While the clip won't play back at 1x (normal) speed, every frame plays back, with no frames dropped.
> Correct: SD video uses interlaced scanning, whereas HD formats may use either interlaced or progressive scanning.
> Incorrect: SD video uses interlaced scanning, while HD formats may use either interlaced or progressive scanning.

### who, whom
Always use *who* as subject of a verb or as subject complement. Always use *whom* as object of a verb or preposition.

> Who is the new system administrator?
> This manual is intended for the person who manages the network.
> Be selective about the people to whom you give network access.

### wish
Don't use; use *want*. See also [want](#want).

### unmount (v.)
In user materials, avoid for making a disk or disk image unavailable. Use alternatives such as *eject* or *make unavailable*, or describe what user must do to make disk unavailable.

> Avoid: Unmount the disc when you finish using it.
> Preferable: Eject the disc when you finish using it.

In server materials or materials for more technical users, *unmount* is OK.

Don't use *unmount* as an intransitive verb.

> Correct: Unmount the volume.
> Incorrect: The volume unmounts.

See also [mount (v.)](#mount-v).

### want
Follow the verb *want* with an object.

> Correct: Make a copy if you want a duplicate. [The word *duplicate* is the object.]
> Correct: Sign up if you want to attend. [The infinitive, *to attend*, is the object.]

Often you can avoid the issue entirely. For example, change *If you want to introduce alternative methods* to *To introduce alternative methods*.

### user
If your audience is users, avoid this term. Address the reader as *you*.

> Correct: You can make movies with effects and a soundtrack.
> Incorrect: Users can make movies with effects and a soundtrack.

When the audience is developers or administrators, use *user* for end users and *you* to address the developer or administrator.

> You may need to prevent users from accidentally making changes.
> You can give users access to the server.

*User* also fits in the context of macOS users, for example when discussing Users & Groups settings in macOS.

See also [end user (n.), end-user (adj.)](#end-user-n-end-user-adj).

### end user (n.), end-user (adj.)
Avoid in favor of *user*. See also [user](#user).

### deselect
OK when you mean *cancel a selection*. Not *uncheck*, *unselect*, *unhighlight*, or *dehighlight*. See also [checkbox](ui-elements.md#checkbox); [radio button](ui-elements.md#radio-button); [unselected (adj.)](#unselected-adj).

### unselected (adj.)
Use for something not selected. Not *deselected*, *unchecked*, or *dehighlighted*. See also [checkbox](ui-elements.md#checkbox); [deselect](#deselect); [unhighlighted (adj.)](#unhighlighted-adj).

### unhighlighted (adj.)
Don't use; use *not highlighted*. See also [unselected (adj.)](#unselected-adj).

### unhighlight (v.)
Don't use. Use *deselect* for clicking to remove highlighting.

### highlighted (adj.)
Not *hilighted*. Don't use *inverted* except in developer materials. When explaining highlighting, use *a highlighted icon changes color* or *a highlighted icon is filled in*.

> Correct: When you click the icon, it becomes highlighted.
> Incorrect: When you click the icon, it highlights.

Don't use *unhighlighted* or *dehighlighted* for an item that isn't highlighted; use *not highlighted*.

### highlighting (n.)
Don't use in user materials. OK in developer materials.

> When an app displays a selection range, it marks the range with highlighting.

### gestures
Finger movements on touchscreen or trackpad or with Apple Vision Pro. Don't call them *finger gestures*; use *gestures*.

In instructions that include gestures, generally don't include the word *finger*.

> Correct: Swipe left or right.
> Incorrect: Swipe your finger left or right.

Referring to fingers is OK if the gesture involves multiple fingers or is complex.

> Triple-tap with two fingers.
> Touch and hold the object with one finger, and use another finger to swipe in the direction you want the object to move.

Also OK to refer to fingers for more detail when first defining or describing a gesture; later occurrences can omit *finger*.

> Defining reference: Pinch two fingers together or apart to zoom out or in.
> Other occurrences: Pinch to zoom in on the page.

For scrolling through documents and other items, *scroll* is often preferable to specific gestures such as *drag* or *swipe*.

See also [drag](#drag); [pinch (v.)](#pinch-v); [rotate](#rotate); [scroll](#scroll); [swipe (n., v.)](#swipe-n-v); [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v); [touch](#touch); [touch and hold](#touch-and-hold).

### drag
Action on desktop or laptop computer, touchscreen device, or Apple Vision Pro.

- *On desktop and laptop computers:* Drag means positioning pointer, pressing and holding mouse or trackpad, moving mouse (or, on trackpad, user's finger), then releasing. For novice users, you might need to define *drag* on first mention.

Use *drag* for actions users perform on onscreen objects. Don't use *drag the mouse* or *drag the pointer*.

> Correct: Drag the Volume slider to change the volume.
> Correct: Choose Tools > Annotate > Rectangle, and then drag to create a rectangle.
> Incorrect: Drag the mouse to draw a rectangle.

Don't use *click and drag*. Don't use *place*, *put*, or *move* when you mean *drag*.

> Correct: Drag your songs into the order you want.
> Correct: Drag the icon to the Trash.
> Incorrect: Click and drag the icon to the Trash.
> Incorrect: Put the icon in the Trash.

- *On devices with touchscreens:* Drag means moving one finger across the screen.

Users can drag to scroll through items, drag controls, or drag to pan around items such as photos and webpages.

> Drag up or down to scroll through your contacts.

Don't use *tap and drag*. Don't say *drag your finger*.

- *On Apple Vision Pro:* Drag means pinching and holding thumb and finger together to select an object, then moving hand to drag it. In user instructions, use *pinch and drag* (not just *drag*) in most cases.

See also [gestures](#gestures); [swipe (n., v.)](#swipe-n-v).

### pinch (v.)
Gesture on touchscreen or trackpad device, or on Apple Vision Pro.

- *On devices with touchscreens or trackpads:* Pinch means placing two fingers on touchscreen or trackpad, then moving them closer together or farther apart.

> Pinch the photo to zoom in or out.

In most cases, *pinch* alone works. For more detail, use *pinch open* for moving fingers apart and *pinch closed* for moving them together. (Developer materials use *pinch close*.)

- *On Apple Vision Pro:* Pinch means pinching and holding thumb and finger together.

Use *pinch and hold* for pinching an interface element or control until more options appear.

Use *pinch and drag* for pinching an object and dragging to move it. Use *pinch and scroll* for pinching and scrolling through content, lists of options, and so on.

### rotate
Placing two fingers slightly apart on a touchscreen or trackpad and twisting them clockwise or counterclockwise.

> Rotate to change the photo's orientation.

Don't use for turning the Digital Crown; use *turn*.

### swipe (n., v.)
On touchscreen or trackpad devices, *swipe* means quickly sliding one or more fingers across touchscreen or trackpad. On Apple Vision Pro, *swipe* means pinching thumb and finger together and quickly flicking wrist.

Users swipe to navigate documents, webpages, and interface areas, and to browse lists and groups of items. Include swipe direction (up, down, left, or right) if needed to describe the action.

> Tap the movie, and then swipe to choose a duration.
> Swipe left to read the next story.
> Swipe down from the top-right corner to open Control Center.

Prepositions such as *over* and *on* can clarify where to swipe.

For swiping with more than one finger, say, for example, *swipe with two fingers* (verb form) or *two-finger swipe* (noun form).

### touch
In visionOS, *touch* is OK for interacting directly with virtual objects (such as the virtual keyboard).

> To type on the virtual keyboard, touch the keys; touch and hold a key to show special characters and accents.

See also [tap (n., v.)](#tap-n-v).

### sync, synced, syncing
Not *synch*, *synched*, or *synching*.

Generally, *syncing* means copying content directly between two devices. Use *sync* for syncing devices or the content on them.

Don't use *synchronize* or *synchronization* as alternatives to *sync* or to explain *sync*.

> Correct: Make sure syncing is turned on for the device.
> Incorrect: Make sure synchronization is turned on for the device.

### output (n., adj.)
Avoid as a verb; use *write to*, *display on*, *print on*, or *print to*.

### output (use)
Don't use as a verb.

### long press (n.), long-press (adj.)
In developer materials, *long press* is OK for the gesture recognizer that detects when a user touches and holds an onscreen item.

Don't use *long press* in user materials. Use *press and hold* for pressing a mechanical button until an action or result occurs. Use *touch and hold* for touching a touchscreen and leaving the finger motionless until an action or result occurs.

See also [press and hold](#press-and-hold); [touch and hold](#touch-and-hold).
