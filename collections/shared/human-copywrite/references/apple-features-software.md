# Apple Features and Software

Apple software, services, OS features, and app names: macOS, iOS, iPadOS, Apple Music, Siri, FaceTime, Apple Intelligence, accessibility features, and built-in apps.

## Entries

### accessibility
Features that help people with disabilities use Apple products and services. Capitalize accessibility feature names. For example:

> Accessibility Keyboard, AssistiveTouch, Guided Access, Hover Text, Invert Colors, Larger Dynamic Type, Live Listen, Magnifier, Safari Reader, Speak Screen, Speak Selection, Switch Control, Text to Speech, Type to Siri, Typing Feedback, Voice Control, VoiceOver, Zoom

### access point
Also *wireless access point*. Device (such as a base station) that sends and receives data in a wireless network. See also [AirPort](apple-products.md#airport).

### Active Noise Cancellation
AirPods feature. Use *noise cancellation* in general references.

> You can switch between Active Noise Cancellation and Transparency mode.
> When the fit of your AirPods is right, you experience better noise cancellation.

OK to capitalize *Noise Cancellation* to match interface text.

> Click the AirPods icon in the menu bar, then click Noise Cancellation.

After first reference, OK to abbreviate *Active Noise Cancellation* as *ANC* in AirPods model names.

> AirPods 4 with Active Noise Cancellation feature a wireless charging case.
> AirPods 4 (ANC) feature a wireless charging case.

### Activity rings
Capitalize names of rings tracking daily activity in the Activity app (for example, *Stand ring*, *Exercise ring*).

### Adaptive Audio
AirPods feature. Don't call it *Adaptive mode*.

> Correct: You can customize Adaptive Audio to allow more or less noise.
> Incorrect: You can customize Adaptive mode to allow more or less noise.

### administrator (n.)
Person with administrative authority over networks, servers, databases, or other IT assets. Don't shorten to *admin*.

Avoid the noun *administrator* alone for a person with a macOS administrator account; keep them distinct from professional administrators. Say *a user with an administrator account*—typically the owner (or owners) of a particular Mac computer.

> Correct: To obtain a static IP address for your computer, contact your network administrator.
> Correct: You must have an administrator account to modify other user accounts on a Mac.
> Incorrect: You must be an administrator to install software.

See also [administrator (adj.)](#administrator-adj); [System Administrator](#system-administrator).

### administrator (adj.)
In macOS, describes the primary account (or accounts) that can create other accounts, set access privileges, install software, and make systemwide settings. OK to shorten to *admin* in server and training materials. Don't use possessive: *administrator's account*, *administrator's password*, or *admin's permissions*.

> Correct: Log in with an administrator account to install software.
> Correct: To give a user broad privileges on a Mac, create an admin account for the user.
> Incorrect: Log in with an administrator's account to install software.

An *administrator computer* runs macOS or macOS Server and manages remote servers.

See also [administrator (n.)](#administrator-n); [standard user](#standard-user); [System Administrator](#system-administrator).

### Apple Account
Account for all Apple services, including the App Store, iCloud, iMessage, and FaceTime. Formerly *Apple ID*.

> Correct: An Apple Account gives you access to all Apple services.
> Incorrect: An Apple ID gives you access to all Apple services.

People sign in *to* their Apple Account; they don't sign in *with* it to devices, stores, or other resources.

> Correct: Sign in to your Apple Account on all your devices.
> Incorrect: Sign in with your Apple Account on all your devices.

Where *Apple ID* meant the email address or phone number tied to an account, replace it with *email address or phone number* (not *Apple Account*).

> Correct: Enter your email address or phone number, and then enter your password.
> Incorrect: Enter your Apple Account, and then enter your password.

If context leaves unclear which email address or phone number, use wording like *your Apple Account email address or phone number* or *the email address or phone number associated with your Apple Account*.

OK to shorten *Apple Account* to *account* to save space or avoid repetition.

> After you sign in to your Apple Account, you can update your account settings.

Don't use terms like *iCloud account*, *iTunes Store account*, or *App Store account*. People have only an Apple Account and sign in to it for services like iCloud, the iTunes Store, and the App Store.

See also [child's Apple Account](#childs-apple-account); [Managed Apple Account](#managed-apple-account).

### app names
Follow the software's own capitalization and spacing—for example, *TextEdit*, *Image Capture*, *DigitalColor Meter*, *iMovie*. In general, don't use *the* with app names (*the Finder* is an exception).

> Correct: Open QuickTime Player.
> Incorrect: Open the QuickTime Player.

### app, application, program
App (short for *application*): computer program that performs a specific task, such as a word processor or game.

Use *app* for iOS, iPadOS, tvOS, visionOS, and watchOS apps.

> Tap a photo to open it in the Photos app.

For Mac software, *app* instead of *application* is usually OK; be consistent within a document.

To distinguish an app's versions for different platforms, use *[app name] for [platform]*.

> Learn about the new features of Pages for iOS and iPadOS.
> You can add tracks to a song in GarageBand for Mac.

In general, use *program* only for software without a graphical user interface.

> The `launchd` program consolidates all system startup activities into a single program.

See also [software development kit](abbreviations-and-acronyms.md#sdk); [system software](#system-software).

### device
Use generically for hardware that connects to a computer (*communications device*, *input device*, *mobile device*, *peripheral device*).

Also use *device*:

- For a category of hardware products: *iOS device*, *iPadOS device*, *iOS and iPadOS devices*, *Android device*
- For more than one of a specific device (avoids pluralizing a trademarked name)

> Correct: If you have multiple iPad devices, you can…
> Incorrect: If you have multiple iPads, you can…

- For Mac computers together with iPhone, iPad, and other devices when no distinction is needed

> iCloud keeps your documents updated across all your devices.
> When you add a photo on your Mac, it appears on your other devices.

In user materials, usually name the specific device (such as *Mac* or *Apple Watch*). In service documentation, if a device name repeats often and context makes the device clear, OK to shorten to *device* or another acceptable short form—for example, *computer* instead of *Mac*, or *watch* instead of *Apple Watch*.

See also [mobile device](#mobile-device); [product](#product); *unit*.

A more general term often works, such as *computer* or *storage space*.

> You can import media files stored on your computer.
> You can delete files to free up storage space.

In user materials, don't omit the article in phrases such as *to a disk* or *on a disk*.

> Correct: You can copy the files to a disk.
> Incorrect: You can copy the files to disk.

For optical media (such as CDs and DVDs), use *disc*.

See also [volume (disk)](#disk).

See also [plurals](punctuation.md#plurals); [trademarks (usage)](punctuation.md#trademarks-usage); and entries for specific product names.
