# Numbers and Dates

How to write numbers, ranges, ordinals, fractions, percentages, dates, times, days of week, months, years, and decades, from the Apple Style Guide.

## Entries

### 2-byte character
Don't use; use *double-byte character*.

### 2D
Abbreviation for *two-dimensional*. No hyphen in the abbreviation. For spelling out abbreviations, see [abbreviations and acronyms](abbreviations-and-acronyms.md#abbreviations-and-acronyms).

### 2K, 4K, 5K, 6K, 8K (adj.)
Horizontal screen or image resolution, in thousands of pixels. Don't put a space between numeral and *K*.

> The 27-inch iMac has a Retina 5K display.
> Apple ProRes codecs support all standard frame sizes (including SD, HD, 2K, and 4K).

### 3D
Abbreviation for *three-dimensional*. No hyphen in the abbreviation. For spelling out abbreviations, see [abbreviations and acronyms](abbreviations-and-acronyms.md#abbreviations-and-acronyms).

### 24/7
Not *24x7*. Spelled out: *24 hours a day, 7 days a week*; *a 24-hour-a-day, 7-day-a-week support program*.

### a.m.
Note periods: *8:30 a.m.* Use a space before the abbreviation.

### dates
Follow these for dates unless your department uses international style (see [Dates and times](international-style.md#dates-and-times)).

- *Commas:* Use a comma between day of month and year.

> June 9, 2025

With a full date, put a comma after the year.

> on June 9, 2025, at 10:00 a.m.

With only month and year, don't use commas.

> in June 2025 at WWDC

- *Cardinal and ordinal numbers:* Cardinals (*1, 2, 3*) in dates with the month; ordinals (*1st, 2nd, 3rd*) in dates without it. Ordinals use full-size letters, not superscript.

> Correct: The conference was held on August 12.
> Correct: The conference was held on the 12th.
> Incorrect: The conference was held on the 12ᵗʰ.

- *When to spell out:* Spell out day and month names in text. Use numerals for the year except at the start of a sentence; there, spell out the year or rewrite to avoid.

- *Slashes in dates:* Don't use the form *3/5/22*, because American and European usage differ.

### daylight saving time
Not *daylight savings time*.

### decades
See [numbers](#numbers).

### degrees
OK to spell out *degrees* or use the degree symbol (°), but be consistent. Hyphenate before a noun: *160-degree viewing angle*. Close up the symbol with the number: *160° viewing angle*, *32° F*. To type the degree symbol, press Option-K.

### fractions
In user materials, spell out fractions with denominator 10 or lower, except in specification lists, technical appendixes, or tables. Hyphenate spelled-out forms: *one-tenth*, *one-fifth*, *three-fourths*.

> This app uses one-fifth of available memory.
> The other app uses only 1/20 of available memory.

For a noninteger greater than 1 in fractional form, use a mixed numeral, not an improper fraction.

> Correct: 1 1/6
> Incorrect: 7/6

### generation
Products released at a particular time or sharing a common design or features. Hyphenate compound adjectives with *generation*: *sixth-generation iPad mini*, *second-generation chip*, *previous-generation Mac Studio*. When generation follows the name, use the numeral: *iPad (5th generation)*. Don't shorten *generation* to *gen* or *G*—for example, *10th-gen iPad* or *iPad 9G*.

If a list mixes models identified by chip and models identified by generation, use this format:

> iPad (8th, 9th, and 10th generation) and iPad (A16)
> iPad mini (5th and 6th generation) and iPad mini (A17 Pro)

See also [iPad](apple-products.md#ipad); [iPod](apple-products.md#ipod).

### numbers
Spell out these numbers:

- Cardinal numbers one through nine. (But use a numeral, however small, for numbers as numbers and for units of measure.)

> You can use your purchases on up to five computers.
> For example, if you have four new messages, the number 4 appears on the app icon.

- Ordinal numbers zero through nine.

> You can install drives in the second, third, and fourth drive bays.

- Numbers at the start of a sentence. (Try to rephrase to avoid starting a sentence with a number.)

> Correct: Two hundred fifty functions are available in the Function Browser.
> Preferable: The Function Browser gives you access to 250 functions.

- A number next to another number, if it helps readability.

> There are sixteen 32-bit registers.

Use numerals:

- For numbers as numbers.

> The numeral 8 occurs eight times.

- For a specific address, bit, byte, chapter, field, key, pin, sector, slot, or track, or for amounts of memory.

> Open the ejectors on slots 1, 2, and 3.
> 8-pin minicircular connector
> Your Mac Pro comes with a minimum of 16 GB of memory.

- For ordinal numbers larger than nine (add *st*, *nd*, *rd*, or *th* to the numeral as appropriate).

- For numbers of the same category in a paragraph, if any number is larger than nine.

> We have 25 computers and 4 printers on the network. [*Computers* and *printers* are the same category.]
> There are two kinds of 32-bit registers. [*Kinds of registers* and *bits* are different categories.]

- For units of measure, including units of time, however small the number. For the unit list, see [Names and unit symbols](units-of-measure.md#names-and-unit-symbols).

> Set the timer for 3 minutes.
> Apple Music 1 is a worldwide radio station that's available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
> You can't set paragraph margins of less than 1 inch.
> If you increase the clip's speed to 200 percent, Final Cut Pro removes frames and the clip plays back in only 2.5 seconds.

- For approximations.

> Cocoa includes definitions for more than 250 additional classes.

More number guidelines:

- *Ranges of numbers:* Use an en dash between endpoints of a continuous range: *bits 3–17*. Use the full concluding number in a range.

> Correct: 2013–2019
> Incorrect: 2013–19

- *Commas:* Use a comma to set off numbers of five digits or more. (If your editor asked for international style, see [Decimals](international-style.md#decimals).)

> 1024
> 65,536

Don't use a comma in memory addresses or in numbers representing microprocessors.

> $FFFF FFFF
> 68020 microprocessor

- *Plural:* Pluralize a number by adding an *s*.

> 1s and 5s
> the '90s

- *Software version numbers:* Use *later* and *earlier* with software version numbers. For example, *macOS 10.13 or later*. See also [version number](apple-features-software.md#version-number).

- *Code values in text:* Use numerals for numeric values in text, except *zero* in the same sentence as *nonzero*.

> ord(blue) returns 0.
> The function fseek returns nonzero for improper seeks; otherwise, it returns zero.

- *Large numbers:* For very large powers of 2, you can use exponential form to avoid a long digit string—for example, 2³². Numbers larger than 65,536 (2¹⁶) are good candidates for exponential form. If one discussion has numbers both larger and smaller than 65,536, show the smaller numbers both ways.

> x must be between 1024 (2¹⁰) and 2³².

See also [fractions](#fractions).

### ordinals

### percent
Always preceded by a numeral, however small the value.

> 1 percent

- *Percent symbol:* OK to use the percent symbol (%) instead of *percent* in technical appendixes, specification lists, and tables, or when repeating the word in a passage gets cumbersome.
- *Nonspecific quantities:* For a nonspecific quantity, use *percentage*, as in *a small percentage of the population*.

### p.m.
Note periods: *6:30 p.m.* Use a space before the abbreviation.

### time of day
Follow these for times unless your department uses international style (see [Dates and times](international-style.md#dates-and-times)):

- *Numerals:* Use numerals for times of day.

> 2:00, 4:15, 7:30

- *a.m. and p.m.:* Include *a.m.* and *p.m.* (lowercase, with periods, preceded by a space) as needed.

> 10:45 a.m., 6:30 p.m.

- *Noon and midnight:* Use *12:00 noon* and *12:00 midnight* or just *noon* and *midnight*.
- *Range of times:* In text, prefer *to* for a range of times.

> 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m., 1:30 to 3:00 p.m.

Otherwise, for example in an event agenda or course listing, use an en dash (Option-Hyphen).

> 10:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m.

If both times are a.m. or both p.m., the first abbreviation is optional, depending on design.

> 1:30–3:00 p.m. *or* 1:30 p.m.–3:00 p.m.

Don't use *from* with the en dash.

> Correct: from 1:30 to 3:00
> Incorrect: from 1:30–3:00

### time zone
Lowercase time zone names except proper nouns.

> Pacific standard time (PST), Pacific daylight time (PDT), mountain standard time (MST), mountain daylight time (MDT), central standard time (CST), central daylight time (CDT), eastern standard time (EST), eastern daylight time (EDT)
